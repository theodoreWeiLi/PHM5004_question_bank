### 1. Achondroplasia (Autosomal Dominant)

Question:&#x20;
John has Achondroplasia, an autosomal dominant disorder, and his partner Mary does not. What is the probability that their first child will inherit Achondroplasia?

Options:&#x20;
A. 0%
B. 25%
C. 50% ✅
D. 75%
E. 100%
F. Not possible to determine given the information
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
Achondroplasia is autosomal dominant. John is likely heterozygous (Aa), and Mary is homozygous normal (aa). Their child has a 50% chance of inheriting the A allele and being affected.

### 2. Cystic Fibrosis (Autosomal Recessive)

Question:&#x20;
Alice and Bob, both carriers of cystic fibrosis, have three children: one without the disorder or carrier status, one with the disorder, and one carrier. What is the probability that their next child will have cystic fibrosis?

Options:&#x20;
A. 0%
B. 25% ✅
C. 50%
D. 75%
E. 100%
F. Not possible to determine given the information
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
Each child has a 25% chance of being affected (aa), 50% chance of being a carrier (Aa), and 25% chance of being unaffected (AA). Previous children do not affect future probabilities.

### 3. Incontinentia Pigmenti (X-linked Dominant)

Question:&#x20;
Emma has Incontinentia Pigmenti, an X-linked dominant disorder. Her partner, John, does not have the disorder. They are expecting another child. What is the probability that this child, if a daughter, will inherit Incontinentia Pigmenti from Emma?

Options:&#x20;
A. 0%
B. 25%
C. 50% ✅
D. 75%
E. 100%
F. Not possible to determine given the information
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
Emma has one mutant X chromosome. Each daughter has a 50% chance of inheriting the affected X and expressing the disorder.

### 4. Hemophilia A (X-linked Recessive)

Question:&#x20;
David, who is normal, and Sarah, a carrier of X-linked recessive Hemophilia A, have a daughter, Emily. They are expecting another child. What is the probability that this child, if a son, will inherit Hemophilia A?

Options:&#x20;
A. 0%
B. 25%
C. 50% ✅
D. 75%
E. 100%
F. Not possible to determine given the information
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
Sarah (XᴴXʰ) can pass either Xᴴ or Xʰ. Sons inherit Y from David and X from Sarah. So there's a 50% chance the son inherits Xʰ and has Hemophilia A.

### 5. Retinitis Pigmentosa (Y-linked)

Question:&#x20;
Michael with Y-linked Retinitis Pigmentosa and his partner, Lisa, have a daughter, Sophie. They are now expecting another child. What is the probability that this child, if a son, will inherit Retinitis Pigmentosa from Michael?

Options:&#x20;
A. 0%
B. 25%
C. 50%
D. 75%
E. 100% ✅
F. Not possible to determine given the information
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
Y-linked disorders are passed from father to son via the Y chromosome. All sons of affected fathers will inherit the disorder.

### 6. Metabolic Syndrome (Polygenic + Lifestyle)

Question:&#x20;
A patient presents with high blood pressure, increased waist circumference, and elevated fasting glucose levels. This patient has a family history of similar symptoms in both paternal and maternal lineages. What is the genetic basis of this disorder?

Options:&#x20;
A. Deletion of a part of chromosome 5
B. Trinucleotide repeat expansion in the ATXN1 gene
C. Homozygous mutation in the HFE gene
D. Mutation in the mitochondrial MT-ND4 gene
E. Multiple genetic variants combined with lifestyle factors, characteristic of Metabolic Syndrome ✅
F. None of the Above
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
Metabolic syndrome is multifactorial—caused by polygenic risk and environmental factors like diet and exercise. The other options refer to unrelated monogenic disorders.

### 7. Huntington’s Disease (Autosomal Dominant)

Question:&#x20;
A 40-year-old patient exhibits progressive motor dysfunction, cognitive decline, and psychiatric symptoms, with a family history of similar but later-onset symptoms in earlier generations. What is the genetic basis of this disorder?

Options:&#x20;
A. Multiple genetic variants combined with environmental factors
B. Expansion of CAG trinucleotide repeats in the HTT gene ✅
C. Mutation in the CFTR gene
D. Microdeletion in the 22q11.2 region
E. Mutation in the mitochondrial MT-ND4 gene
F. None of the Above
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
Huntington’s disease is caused by CAG repeat expansion in the HTT gene. The anticipation effect explains earlier onset in subsequent generations.

### 8. Angelman Syndrome (Imprinting Disorder)

Question:&#x20;
Angelman syndrome is a rare neurogenetic disorder characterized by severe intellectual and developmental disability. What is the genetic basis of this disorder?

Options:&#x20;
A. Duplication of the paternal allele of the SNRPN gene
B. Mutation in the paternal allele of the UBE3A gene
C. Deletion of the maternal allele of the UBE3A gene ✅
D. Microdeletion in the 22q11.2 region
E. Mutation in the mitochondrial MT-ND4 gene
F. None of the Above
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
UBE3A is maternally expressed in the brain. Deletion of the maternal allele leads to Angelman syndrome. Paternal allele is silenced.

### 9. MELAS (Mitochondrial Disorder)

Question:&#x20;
A patient presents with muscle weakness, exercise intolerance, and hearing loss with elevated lactate levels. Family history shows maternal transmission of similar symptoms. What is the genetic basis of this disorder?

Options:&#x20;
A. Mutation in the mitochondrial MT-ND1 gene ✅
B. Deletion of a segment on chromosome 15
C. Expansion of CAG repeats in the HTT gene
D. Microdeletion in the 22q11.2 region
E. Mutation in the LMNA gene
F. None of the Above
G. Don't Know

Analysis:&#x20;
MELAS is caused by mitochondrial mutations, often in MT-ND1. Mitochondrial inheritance is strictly maternal, matching the family history.
